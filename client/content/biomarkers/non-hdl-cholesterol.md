## What it measures

Non-HDL cholesterol is your total cholesterol with the HDL taken away. What is left is all
the cholesterol being carried by the particles associated with deposits in blood vessels —
LDL and several others that a standard panel does not list separately.

Your lab does not measure it with its own test. It subtracts one number on the panel from
another, which means it is available on any report that has both, and it needs no extra blood.

## What high non-HDL cholesterol means

A value above the range printed on your report means more cholesterol is being carried by
those particles than that lab uses as its comparison point. Because it captures more than LDL
alone, some clinicians find it a more complete single number, particularly when triglycerides
are raised.

## What low non-HDL cholesterol means

A value below the range is usually not a concern on its own. As with the rest of the panel,
your doctor reads it alongside your other results rather than by itself.

## What can affect the result

Everything that moves total cholesterol or HDL moves this number too, because it is built
from both: diet, weight, activity, smoking, alcohol, age, family history, some medicines,
pregnancy and recent illness. It does not depend on the calculation that labs use for LDL,
which is one of the reasons it is sometimes preferred.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Do you use non-HDL or LDL as the main number for me, and why?
- What does this result mean alongside the rest of my panel?
- Should we repeat the test, and when?
- Would changes to food or activity be likely to make a difference for me?
