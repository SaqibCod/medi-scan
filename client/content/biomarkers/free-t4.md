## What it measures

T4, or thyroxine, is the main hormone your thyroid makes. Most of it travels through the
blood attached to carrier proteins and is unavailable while attached. The small unattached
share is the part that can enter tissue and do something, and that is what "free" T4 means.

Measuring the free portion is more useful than measuring the total, because anything that
changes the amount of carrier protein — pregnancy, the contraceptive pill — changes the total
without changing how much hormone is actually working.

A free T4 on its own cannot sort out a thyroid problem, which is why it is nearly always
ordered with TSH.

## What high free T4 means

A value above the range printed on your report means more active thyroid hormone is
circulating than that lab's comparison point, which points towards an overactive thyroid. It
can also follow taking more thyroid medicine than the body needs.

## What low free T4 means

A value below the range means less active hormone than that lab's comparison point, which
points towards an underactive thyroid. Read alongside TSH, the pair indicates whether the
issue is with the thyroid itself or with the pituitary gland directing it.

## What can affect the result

A serious illness unrelated to the thyroid shifts thyroid results for a time. Pregnancy
changes both the hormone levels and the ranges that apply. Several medicines affect it,
including the contraceptive pill, steroids and amiodarone. Biotin supplements can interfere
with the measurement itself on some analysers.

Different labs use different methods for free T4, and their results are not directly
interchangeable — which is one reason to compare against the range on your own report rather
than against an older one from elsewhere.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- What do my free T4 and TSH mean together?
- Could a recent illness, pregnancy or medicine have affected this?
- Should it be repeated, and at the same lab?
- Would thyroid antibodies add anything?
