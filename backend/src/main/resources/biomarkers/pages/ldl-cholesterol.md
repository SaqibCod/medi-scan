## What it measures

LDL stands for low-density lipoprotein. It is one of the particles that carry cholesterol
through your blood, taking it from the liver out to cells around the body that need it.

It is often called "bad" cholesterol because when there is more of it than the body has use
for, the surplus can build up in the walls of blood vessels. HDL, the "good" one, works in
the other direction and helps carry cholesterol back to the liver.

## What high LDL means

A value above the range printed on your report means your LDL is higher than that lab's
comparison point. Over many years, high LDL is linked to plaque building up in the arteries,
which raises the risk of heart disease and stroke.

A single result is a snapshot. Your doctor reads it together with your other results, your
history and your other risk factors, and may want to repeat the test before deciding
anything.

## What low LDL means

A value below the range is usually not a concern on its own. Very low values are less common
and can be linked to some medicines or health conditions. Your doctor can tell you whether it
matters in your case.

## What can affect the result

Recent meals, a recent illness, pregnancy and some medicines can shift the number, which is
why some labs ask you to fast first. Labs also calculate or measure LDL in different ways:
many work it out from the other values on the panel rather than measuring it directly, and
that is one reason their ranges differ.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Is my LDL something we should act on now, or watch?
- Should we repeat the test, and when?
- How do my other results and my family history change what this number means?
- Would changes to food or activity make a difference for me?
