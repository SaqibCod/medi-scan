## What it measures

Glucose is a sugar and your body's main fuel. It comes from the food you eat, and insulin —
a hormone from the pancreas — moves it out of the blood and into cells where it is used.

A blood glucose test is a single reading of how much is circulating at the moment the sample
was taken. It is used to screen for diabetes and prediabetes, to look into symptoms that
might be caused by blood sugar that is too high or too low, and to keep an eye on medicines
known to affect it.

## What high glucose means

A value above the range printed on your report means more sugar was circulating than that
lab's comparison point. Repeatedly high results are how diabetes and prediabetes are found,
but a single high reading is not a diagnosis — it is a reason to look further.

It matters enormously whether you had eaten. A result taken after a meal is expected to be
higher than a fasting one, and many reports print a range that assumes you fasted. If the
sample was not a fasting one, the printed range may not be the right comparison at all.

## What low glucose means

A value below the range means less sugar was circulating than that lab's comparison point.
This can happen with some diabetes medicines, after a long gap without food, with alcohol, or
with conditions affecting the liver or hormones. Low readings that come with symptoms —
shakiness, sweating, confusion — are worth telling your doctor about promptly.

## What can affect the result

When you last ate, and what, is the largest influence. Beyond that: physical activity, stress
from a serious illness, surgery or injury, pregnancy, and a long list of medicines, including
steroids, some antipsychotics and some blood pressure treatments. Because a single reading
moves so much, your doctor may repeat it or use a test that reflects a longer period.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Was this a fasting sample, and does the range on my report assume one?
- Should it be repeated, or followed with a different test?
- Could any of my medicines be affecting this number?
- What symptoms should make me get in touch?
