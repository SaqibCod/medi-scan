## What it measures

White blood cells, also called leukocytes, are your immune system's cells. They circulate in
the blood and move into tissue to deal with infection and injury.

This test counts all of them together. There are several different types doing different
jobs, and a test called a differential breaks the total down by type — which is usually
more informative than the total alone.

## What high white cells means

A value above the range printed on your report is called leukocytosis. The everyday reason is
that the body is fighting something: an infection, or inflammation somewhere. It also rises
with allergic reactions, after injury or surgery, with some medicines including steroids, and
with smoking, stress and pregnancy.

Much less commonly a very high count reflects a condition of the blood or bone marrow. The
differential and the rest of the report usually make clear which kind of picture it is.

## What low white cells means

A value below the range is called leukopenia. It can follow some viral infections, and is
expected with chemotherapy and some other medicines that affect the bone marrow. Autoimmune
conditions and some long-term infections can cause it.

A count that sits slightly below the printed range is common and often means nothing at all,
particularly as some healthy people simply run low.

## What can affect the result

Time of day makes a small difference. Recent exercise, stress, smoking and pregnancy raise
it. Steroids raise it. Chemotherapy and some other medicines lower it. A recent infection can
leave it unsettled for a while after you feel better.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Does the differential suggest which type of cell is driving this?
- Could a recent infection or one of my medicines explain it?
- Should it be repeated once I am well?
- What symptoms should prompt me to get in touch?
